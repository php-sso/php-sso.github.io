One sign-in for everything you run

php-sso is an OpenID Connect provider written in PHP. It signs your people in, asks for a second factor, lets staff of other companies in through SAML, and tells your apps who they are. It starts as one container with no other services, and runs on SQLite, MySQL or PostgreSQL when you want your data in a database server.

Read the documentation See the standards it follows

What you get

All of this is built and managed from the admin panel.

Signing in

Identifier first
Ask who someone is, then how they prove it. Sign in by username, email address, or either.
Two-step verification
An authenticator app with a QR code to scan, and recovery codes. Make it optional, required for administrators, or required for everyone.
Social sign-in
Google, GitHub, or any OpenID Connect provider. People are matched on the provider's own id, never on the email address alone.
Password reset by email
People who forget their password get a link by email. It works once and expires, signs them out everywhere when used, and the emails are templates you can restyle like the sign-in pages.
Steps before sign-in
Make someone choose a new password, or accept a notice you link to, before they can continue. Existing sessions are stopped too.

Companies

SAML
Staff of a customer or partner sign in through their own identity provider, such as Okta or Entra. Their email domain sends them there.
SCIM
The company's system creates, updates and deactivates people and groups here. Deactivating someone ends their sessions at once.

Your apps

OpenID Connect
Authorization code with PKCE, refresh tokens that rotate, device flow, client credentials, and logout that reaches your apps, and is sent again when an app was down.
Groups and roles
Apps receive the groups a person is in and the roles you give them, per app.
Who may use an app
Restrict an app to chosen people or groups, and to chosen network addresses.
How they signed in
Every ID token says whether a password, an outside account or a second factor was used, so an app can require one.

Running it

Admin panel
Users, groups, apps, company connections, notices and sign-in settings, in one place. Long lists have search and pages.
Sign-in protection
See failed attempts as they happen, reset them for one person, and set the limits yourself. Lock accounts and restrict addresses.
Your look
Sign-in and account pages are templates you mount as a folder. Change them without rebuilding.
Docker or Composer
Pull the image from the GitHub container registry, build your own on top of it with your theme, or install it as a PHP project with composer create-project.
Your database
A SQLite file to start with, or MySQL or PostgreSQL. Schema changes are applied by migrations, and an older version refuses to run against a database a newer one has upgraded.

Keeping it safe

Audit log
Who changed what, and when: every change in the admin panel, the changes people make to their own account, and key, backup and restore commands. Search it, keep it as long as you choose, and send it to your log collector as it is written.
Safe by default
A strict Content-Security-Policy and secure cookies. The server refuses to start with plain HTTP on a public address, demo data in production, or a weak first password.
Keys without downtime
Rotate the signing key: the new one is published before it signs, and the old one stays published until the tokens it signed have expired.
Backup and restore
One file with the data and the keys, taken while the server runs. It restores into any of the three databases, which is also how you move from SQLite to PostgreSQL.
Health check
A /healthz address for your load balancer, and a health check built into the Docker image.

See how a sign-in is decided

php-sso asks different things of different people. Try your own team, then a company address, and switch on the second step. What your app receives changes with how the person signed in.

Use alice@example.com to be asked for a password, or sam@acme.com to be sent to a company's identity provider.

sso.example.com/login

A simulation in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the claims are an example, not a signed token.

Your apps need only OpenID Connect

Point any OpenID Connect library at the discovery address and register the app in the admin panel. There is no SDK to install.

The ID token says who signed in and how: sub, email, groups, roles, auth_time and amr. An app that needs a second factor checks that amr contains mfa.

Each app lists the scopes it may ask for. Signing out of one app can end the session in the others.

Issuer
https://sso.example.com
Discovery
https://sso.example.com/.well-known/openid-configuration
Flow
Authorization code with PKCE (S256), required for every app
Scopes
openid profile email groups roles, and any you add
Logout
RP-initiated, plus back-channel logout to apps that support it

Let other companies' people in

A customer or partner signs in with their own identity provider. You add the connection and the email domains it speaks for. Their system can also keep the list of people up to date, so someone who leaves the company is out of your apps too.

SAML sign-in

  • Someone who types an address at the company's domain goes straight to their provider, with no password page.
  • A company can only speak for addresses at its own domains. It cannot vouch for anyone else's.
  • Answers from the provider must be signed, meant for you, recent, and a reply to a sign-in you started.
  • For providers that insist, our requests can be signed too, with a key you can replace without a gap.

SCIM provisioning

  • Tokens are shown once, kept as hashes, and limited to the domains you choose.
  • A SCIM client never touches administrators or people it did not create.
  • Deactivating a person locks the account and ends their sessions at once.
  • Bulk operations load many people at once, with the same rules and limits as one at a time.

Two ways to run it

Use the container image, or install it as a PHP project on your own web server. Either way the settings are environment variables, the data is in one database, and an upgrade is a new version that migrates the schema when it starts.

With Docker you can build your own image FROM ours and add your theme. With Composer you get the files and run them on PHP 8.3 or later behind Apache or nginx.

Docker
docker run ghcr.io/php-sso/php-sso
with a volume for the data, and your address and the first administrator's password as settings
Composer
composer create-project --no-dev php-sso/php-sso
Databases
SQLite, MySQL 8.0.19 or later, PostgreSQL 13 or later; the tests run on all three
Operations
bin/migrate, bin/keys, bin/backup, bin/restore, bin/maintenance and bin/mail-test

Documentation

How to install, configure and run php-sso is in its own documentation, which lives with the code. The three libraries it is built on each have theirs, and you can use them in your own PHP applications. All four are open source under the MIT license.

php-sso
The identity provider: running it with Docker or Composer, setting it up, security (with the threat model and its known gaps), backups, connecting your apps, SAML, SCIM and changing how it looks.
oauth-server
The OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect library underneath, for building your own server.
composer require php-sso/oauth-server
scim-server
SCIM 2.0 provisioning of users and groups, with your own accounts and storage behind it.
composer require php-sso/scim-server
saml
SAML 2.0 sign-in through a company's identity provider, for any PHP application.
composer require php-sso/saml
